## What it is (evidence from the reference, adapted)

A one-payment pack of video editing **skills for Claude Code**. The buyer drops in a raw video, names a style, and Claude does the cut, captions, graphics and render. Skills are plain files the buyer owns and can adjust, combine and extend. The offer, section order and persuasive logic come from the owner's reference page (pasted HTML). The copy is rewritten in Spanish in a new voice. Nothing visual is taken from it.

**Brand (authored):** `CORTE`. Spanish for *the cut*. One word, says the product, and the whole art direction hangs off it.

**Unknowns, kept out of the page as invented facts:** final price, checkout URL, real sample videos, refund policy and the real style list. They live in one config block and in the catalog, and the report lists them.

## The eight topics

1. **Vibe:** a cutting room at midnight. Precision, silence, one red REC light, the rhythm of the cut. References: the editing of *Whiplash* (the cut as tension), the grade of *Blade Runner 2049* (fog, graphite, one colour source) and a Steenbeck flatbed (a physical object made for cutting film).
2. **Journey (from the reference offer):** see the product working → what editing costs you now → the styles (choose) → they are yours to change → how it works → why a template beats a blank page → what you need → price → doubts → buy.
3. **Energy:** a loud cinematic open; cooler and lucid in the cost; the highest point in the styles; a breath in "make them yours"; clear and procedural in "how it works"; calm, bright paper for price; firm and dark at the close.
4. **Feeling, stage by stage:** see the curve below. **The one moment:** you pick a style and the whole page re-edits itself in that style.
5. **What no site they have seen does:** the page is footage. You grade it.
6. **Range:** cinematic premium-minimal: dark graphite, one accent, a hard cut to paper for the price. Chosen because the product is a craft tool for creators who judge taste instantly.
7. **One world or scenes:** scenes. Every seam is a named editing transition. One persistent object (La Cinta) travels the page as the continuity.
8. **Assets:** none supplied. The world is rendered: WebGL film strip, live Canvas animatics of each style (structure previews, labelled as such) and no stock footage. The owner's real sample videos replace the animatics per style when added.

## Grammar: *Sala de montaje* (new, named)

Why not the eight:
- **Filmic one-shot:** it hides seams, and this product is about seams.
- **Chaptered editorial:** too bookish for a creator tool.
- **Live surface:** the product runs in Claude Code, not in the page, and faking its terminal is banned.
- **Continuous world:** no geography.
- **Typographic poster:** the product is visual.
- **Gallery:** the catalog is only one act of the argument.
- **Split stage:** there is no two-sided argument.
- **Rhythmic cutlist:** it forbids the long peak this page needs.

The rules of *Sala de montaje*:
- **Nav:** a timecode bar. The page is a 90-second edit, and the timecode is your position in it. Clicking it opens the Bin, where sections are listed as clips with their in-points and you jump between them.
- **Sequence:** scenes joined by named editing transitions. No two seams use the same transition.
- **Hero:** the morph object among real-size reels, with the headline between planes.
- **Close:** a render plate that inherits the chosen style.
- **Forbids:** scroll cues, section counters (the timecode is the only position readout), glass as decoration, gradient text, glow, AI purple, cream and brass, custom cursor, em dashes, stock footage posing as client work, and identical-card grids as page structure.

## Signature move: El Grade

Choosing a style (click, Enter, tap) sets `data-estilo` and changes four things at once:
1. The character of La Cinta: rim heat, sheen, twist and noise.
2. The caption treatment of every later H2, from a closed set of four taken from editing: `acento`, `palabra`, `apilado` and `limpio`.
3. The nav readout (`GRADE · <style>`).
4. The close's render plate, which plays that style and carries `?estilo=` to checkout.

The accent never changes hue.
